# Devlyn Style Guide

This guide defines writing conventions, formatting standards, and editorial rules for all Devlyn content — blog posts, service pages, solution pages, LinkedIn posts, outbound emails, and resource assets.

Always reference alongside brand-voice.md for tone guidance.

---

## Grammar & Mechanics

### Capitalization

**Headlines & Subheadings**: Title Case for all H1 and H2 headings. Sentence case for H3 and below.
- ✅ H1: "How to Hire a Senior Laravel Developer from India in 2026"
- ✅ H2: "What to Look for in a Senior Laravel Developer"
- ✅ H3: "Experience level requirements"

**Product / Service Names**:
- Devlyn: Always capitalized
- ViitorCloud: Always capitalized (but rarely mentioned in client-facing content)
- AI-driven development: Hyphenated, lowercase "driven"
- MVP: Always caps
- SaaS: Always caps (not "Saas" or "saas")
- ODC (Offshore Development Center): Caps for abbreviation, title case for full form
- CTO: Always caps
- API: Always caps
- DevOps: Capitalize both D and O

**Technology Names**:
- Laravel (not "laravel" or "LARAVEL")
- React (not "ReactJS" in running text — use "React.js" only when technically specific)
- Node.js (not "NodeJS" or "node")
- React Native (not "RN" or "react-native" in non-code contexts)
- Tailwind CSS (not "tailwind" or "TailwindCSS")
- Python (always capitalized)
- AWS, GCP: Always caps
- GitHub: One capital G, one capital H
- Docker, Kubernetes: Capitalize as proper nouns

### Numbers

**Spell out**: One through nine in running text
**Use numerals**: 10 and above

**Always use numerals for**:
- Percentages: 40% (not "forty percent")
- Dollar amounts: $25/hour (not "twenty-five dollars")
- Time expressions: 6 weeks (not "six weeks") — the specificity of "6 weeks" is a Devlyn brand claim, always numeral
- Experience levels: 5–10+ years (never "five to ten")
- Developer rates: $25–$55/hour (always numeral, always include range)

**Large numbers**:
- Use commas: 10,000 (not 10000)
- Spell out million/billion: $1.2 million (not $1,200,000 in running text)

### Punctuation

**Oxford Comma**: Always use.
- ✅ "Laravel, React, and Node.js engineers"
- ❌ "Laravel, React and Node.js engineers"

**Em Dashes**: Use — with no spaces on either side — for parenthetical statements.
- ✅ "Devlyn's engineers — senior-only, AI-augmented — deploy in days."
- ❌ "Devlyn's engineers - senior-only - deploy in days."

**Hyphens**: Use for compound modifiers before a noun.
- ✅ "AI-driven development", "senior-only team", "product-first thinking"
- ❌ "AI driven development", "senior only team"

**Quotation Marks**: Use double quotes ("like this"). Single quotes only for nested quotes within a quote.

**Colons in headers**: Use sparingly. When used, what follows should be a complete thought.
- ✅ "Build Your MVP in 6 Weeks: Here's How Devlyn Does It"

### Abbreviations & Acronyms

**First use**: Spell out, then abbreviate in parentheses.
- "Offshore Development Center (ODC)"
- "Chief Technology Officer (CTO)"
- Exceptions — universally known: API, SaaS, MVP (no need to spell out)

**Avoid Latin abbreviations** in public-facing content. Write "for example" instead of "e.g.", "that is" instead of "i.e.", "and so on" instead of "etc."

## Word Choice & Usage

### Say This → Not That (Devlyn-Specific)

| Say This | Not That | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| AI-driven development | AI-powered development | "Driven" implies process; "powered" is a hollow buzzword |
| Senior engineers | Resources / talent | Dehumanizing; Devlyn positions on seniority |
| Book a Strategy Call | Contact us / Get in touch | Specific, outcome-oriented CTA |
| Engineering partner | Vendor / agency | Partner implies accountability |
| Dedicated offshore team | Outsourced developers | "Outsourced" carries negative connotation |
| Weekly demos | Regular updates | Demos are concrete; updates are vague |
| Outcome-driven | Results-oriented | More precise in engineering context |
| MVP in 6 weeks | Fast MVPs | Specific is credible; "fast" is a claim anyone can make |
| Product-first thinking | Full-service | Devlyn's differentiator, not a commodity claim |
| 5–10+ years of experience | Experienced team | Specific over vague |
| $25–$55/hour | Affordable rates / competitive pricing | Transparency over hedging |
| India-based engineers | Offshore team | Geographic precision; "offshore" can feel generic |
| Senior-only | Best-in-class / top talent | "Senior-only" is verifiable; "best" is subjective |

### Words Banned from All Devlyn Content

Never use in any client-facing content:
- Cutting-edge
- World-class
- Passionate team
- Best-in-class
- Synergy
- Leverage (as a noun)
- Robust (in generic sense)
- Seamless
- Revolutionary
- Disruptive
- Game-changing
- Innovative solutions
- Committed to excellence
- Trusted partner (without a specific case study to back it)
- "We help businesses grow" (too vague)
- "One-stop shop"
- "End-to-end solutions" (unless referring to a specific defined scope)

### Active Verbs — Use These

Ship, build, deploy, deliver, review, own, reduce, compress, scale, hire, launch, validate, architect, migrate, modernize, rescue

### Words to Cut as Filler

Remove: very, really, truly, actually, importantly, essentially, basically, simply, just, quite, rather

## Formatting Standards

### Text Formatting

**Bold**: Use for key technical terms, named differentiators, and critical data points.
- ✅ "Devlyn deploys **senior engineers only** — no juniors in client-facing roles."
- ✅ "Our **6-week MVP program** includes weekly demos and full codebase handover."
- ❌ Don't bold entire sentences or use bold for decoration

**Italics**: Use sparingly — for technical terms on first introduction, or genuine emphasis where bold would be too heavy.

**ALL CAPS**: Only for established abbreviations (MVP, API, SaaS, CTO, AWS, GCP, ODC)

### Lists

**Bullet lists**: Use for parallel, non-sequential items — features, criteria, red flags, tech stack components
- Capitalize first word of each item
- No period if items are fragments; period if items are complete sentences
- Maintain parallel grammatical structure across all items
- 3–8 items; break into subsections if more

**Numbered lists**: Use for sequential steps — process, methodology, how-to instructions
- Same capitalization and punctuation rules as bullets
- Never number a list of 2 items — use prose instead

**Nested lists**: Maximum one level of nesting. If needing two levels, restructure into subsections.

### Tables

Use tables for:
- Cost/rate comparisons (India vs US vs UK developer rates)
- Engagement model comparisons (dedicated vs project vs hourly)
- Feature comparisons (Devlyn vs offshore agencies vs freelancers vs in-house)
- Technology comparison (React Native vs Flutter, Laravel vs Django)

Table format: Use markdown table syntax. Always include a header row. Align text left. Keep cell content concise.

### Code References

- Inline code (backticks) for: command names, package names, function names, file paths
- Example: "Devlyn's Laravel engineers are proficient in `artisan` commands, `Eloquent` ORM, and `Horizon` queue management."

## Content Structure — Devlyn Blog Posts

### Introduction (150–200 words)
1. **Direct answer or thesis** (first 1–2 sentences — for AI citation)
2. **Problem/stakes** (what goes wrong if this is decided poorly)
3. **Promise** (what this post delivers specifically)
4. **Keyword** (in first 100 words)

### Key Takeaways Block (After Introduction, Before First H2)
Required on every blog post.
```
> **Key Takeaways**
> - [Specific claim with numbers/names]
> - [Specific claim with numbers/names]
> - [Specific claim with numbers/names]
> - [Optional 4th claim]
```

### Body Sections (4–7 H2 sections)
- One clear idea per section
- 250–400 words per H2 section
- Use H3s to break complex sections
- Include tables, bullet lists for scannability

### FAQ Section (Required)
- 4–6 questions per post
- Natural language question format (how real CTOs ask, not keyword-stuffed)
- Direct answer in first sentence of each response, then expand

### Conclusion (150–200 words)
- Restate core takeaway
- Include primary keyword
- End with CTA: "Book a Strategy Call at devlyn.ai/contact"

## Date & Time Style

**Dates**: Month DD, YYYY in full prose (May 7, 2026). In tables or tightly formatted contexts: MM/DD/YYYY.

**Year in titles**: Always include for cost, rate, hiring, and comparison content. "Laravel Developer Hourly Rate 2026" — the year signals freshness and is a ranking factor.

**Time zones**: Specify when relevant — "IST/EST overlap window", "4-hour daily overlap with US Eastern Time".

## Statistics & Data

### Citing External Data
- Always cite with source name and year: "According to Stack Overflow's 2025 Developer Survey..."
- Link to original source where possible
- Round for readability: "over 1.2 million" not "1,247,382"

### Devlyn-Specific Data Points (Use These — They're Defensible)
- Senior engineers: 5–10+ years of experience
- Deployment speed: 3–5 business days for dedicated engineers
- MVP timeline: 6 weeks (Devlyn's structured program)
- Developer rates: $25–$55/hour range (India-based, depends on tech and experience)
- Timezone overlap: Minimum 4 hours daily with US/EU
- Weekly demo cadence: Every Friday, working software

### What Not to Invent
- Specific client names without permission
- Case study numbers without verified project data
- Testimonial quotes that don't exist
- "X% faster" claims without supporting data

## SEO-Specific Style

### Meta Titles
- 50–60 characters
- Primary keyword early in title
- Include year for time-sensitive content
- Format: `[Keyword]: [Benefit] | Devlyn` or `How to [Goal]: [Year] Guide`

### Meta Descriptions
- 150–160 characters exactly — never truncate mid-sentence
- Include primary keyword
- Include specific data point or outcome
- End with CTA action word

### URL Slugs
- Lowercase, hyphens only
- 3–6 words
- Include primary keyword
- Pattern: `/blog/[keyword-phrase]`

## Voice Reminders (Quick Reference)

**Write for**: CTOs and technical founders — not beginners, not marketing teams
**Tone**: Senior, credible, direct, outcome-first
**Persona**: Experienced technical co-founder writing for a peer
**What to avoid**: Corporate speak, generic agency language, hype, vague claims

**Always end blog posts with**: "Book a Strategy Call at devlyn.ai/contact"
**Never end with**: "We hope this was helpful!" or "Feel free to reach out!" or any casual sign-off
