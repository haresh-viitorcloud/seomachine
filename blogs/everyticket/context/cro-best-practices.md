# CRO Best Practices — EveryTicket

Conversion Rate Optimisation guidelines for EveryTicket landing pages, blog CTAs, and demo request flows.

---

## EveryTicket's Conversion Model

EveryTicket sells through a **demo-first B2B model**. There is no free trial. The conversion funnel is:

```
Blog / Search → Landing Page / Blog Article → Demo Request → Sales Call → Paid Plan
```

**Primary conversion action**: Schedule a demo
**Secondary conversion action**: Contact us / Talk to our team

All CRO decisions should push toward demo bookings — not generic sign-ups, downloads, or newsletter subscriptions.

---

## Above the Fold

### The 5-Second Test
A visitor landing on any EveryTicket page must understand within 5 seconds:
1. **What** EveryTicket is — museum and venue ticketing software
2. **Who** it is for — museum administrators, cultural institutions, Indian venues
3. **Why** it matters — specific outcome (faster entry, GST compliance, 60-min setup)
4. **What to do next** — Schedule a demo

### Required Above-the-Fold Elements

| Element | Purpose | EveryTicket Guideline |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | State the core outcome | Benefit-first, under 70 characters |
| Subheadline | Add context | Name the audience and differentiate |
| CTA Button | Drive demo booking | "Schedule a Demo" or "Book a Demo" |
| Trust Signal | Reduce risk | "150,000+ tickets processed" or plan starting price |

### Above-the-Fold Avoid
- Multiple competing CTAs on the same page
- Autoplay video with sound
- Navigation links that lead visitors away from the conversion goal
- Vague headlines ("Welcome to EveryTicket" or "The Best Museum Ticketing Software")
- Generic claims without a number or outcome

## Headline Best Practices

### Headline Formulas for EveryTicket

**Outcome-first:**
- "Sell museum tickets online in 60 minutes"
- "Cut entry queues by 40% with timed ticketing"
- "GST-compliant museum ticketing starting at ₹7,000/month"

**Problem-solution:**
- "Your counters close at 5 pm. Your online booking portal never does."
- "Stop losing revenue to manual ticket reconciliation"
- "No more fake tickets. No more queue chaos."

**Audience-specific:**
- "Museum ticketing software built for Indian institutions"
- "The only ticketing system with built-in UPI and GST billing"
- "From small regional museums to government heritage sites"

**Social proof:**
- "Trusted by museums that have processed over 150,000 tickets"
- "Go live in 60 minutes — like MAP Bangalore did"

### Headline Testing Checklist
- [ ] States a specific outcome or number (not a vague claim)
- [ ] Under 70 characters for mobile
- [ ] Addresses the museum administrator directly (not generic "businesses")
- [ ] Avoids banned constructions ("Not just X, but Y")
- [ ] No em dashes (—)

### Weak Headlines to Avoid
- "Welcome to EveryTicket"
- "The Best Museum Ticketing Solution"
- "We Help Museums Sell Tickets"
- "Your All-in-One Ticketing Platform"
- Any headline starting with "Our" or "We"

## CTA Best Practices

### Primary CTAs (Demo Booking)

| Context | Strong CTA | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Hero section | "Schedule a Demo" | "Sign Up" |
| Hero section | "Book a Free Demo" | "Get Started" |
| Mid-article | "See EveryTicket in action" | "Learn More" |
| Mid-article | "Talk to our team" | "Contact Us" |
| End of article | "Schedule a demo today" | "Click here" |
| End of article | "Get EveryTicket running in 60 minutes" | "Submit" |

**CTA Button Formula**: [Action Verb] + [Specific Benefit or Object]
- "Schedule" + "a Demo"
- "Book" + "Your Free Demo"
- "See" + "EveryTicket in Action"
- "Get" + "Your Museum Live in 60 Minutes"

### CTA Placement in Blog Articles
Per seo-guidelines.md, every blog article has one CTA section placed **before the conclusion**:
- Uses "we" (EveryTicket speaks as a team)
- 2–4 sentences grounded in a specific capability discussed in the article
- Ends with a natural link or button to the demo booking page
- Does not read like a sales pitch — it reads like a logical next step

**Example (for an article about timed entry):**
> Managing visitor flow across time slots is straightforward when the system does it automatically. We built EveryTicket's timed entry feature specifically for Indian museums where weekend footfall peaks are unpredictable. If you want to see how it works for your venue, schedule a demo and we will walk you through a live setup.

### CTA Placement on Landing Pages
1. **Hero CTA** (above fold): Primary, most prominent — "Schedule a Demo"
2. **Post-problem CTA** (after establishing pain): "See how EveryTicket solves this"
3. **Post-proof CTA** (after testimonials / social proof): "Join museums already using EveryTicket"
4. **Closing CTA** (page bottom): Final push with a risk reversal statement nearby

### CTA Visual Rules
- High contrast colour — must stand out from background
- Minimum tap target 44x44px for mobile
- White space around the button
- Full-width on mobile screens
- One CTA style per page — do not mix button styles

## Objection Handling

These are the real objections museum administrators raise. Address them in landing pages, article CTAs, and demo follow-up content.

### "We don't have a technical team to manage this"
- Response: EveryTicket handles setup, configuration, and staff training. Your team needs no technical background. The online portal goes live in 60 minutes with our support.
- Proof point: One-time staff training included with every plan

### "Our internet connection at counters is unreliable"
- Response: EveryTicket's POS operates in offline mode during connectivity loss. All transactions sync automatically when the connection restores — no data is lost.
- Proof point: Offline POS with automatic sync

### "Will it support UPI and GST billing?"
- Response: UPI payments via Pine Labs and PayU are built in natively. Every transaction generates a GST-compliant invoice automatically — no additional setup required.
- Proof point: "GST-ready invoicing and UPI support out of the box"

### "We're a small museum with a limited budget"
- Response: EveryTicket's Starter plan is ₹7,000/month — flat fee, no per-ticket commissions, no hidden costs. At 500 tickets/month, that is ₹14 per visitor for a complete ticketing, POS, and analytics system.
- Proof point: Starts at ₹7,000/month; no per-ticket fees

### "Implementation will take too long and disrupt operations"
- Response: Your online booking portal goes live in 60 minutes. Full deployment — including POS, staff training, and payment gateway integration — follows a 30-day framework with no disruption to daily operations.
- Proof point: "60-minute go-live; full system in 30 days"

### "Are there long-term contracts?"
- Response: No long-term contracts. EveryTicket runs month-to-month. You are not locked in.
- Proof point: "No long-term contracts. No hidden fees."

### "We already use a manual system — will migration be difficult?"
- Response: EveryTicket handles the data migration. Historical visitor data can be imported and the old system runs in parallel during week three of the 30-day deployment framework.
- Proof point: Parallel system run during implementation

### "Is this suitable for government museums?"
- Response: EveryTicket is built to Indian government institution requirements — GST compliance, multi-category pricing (Indian nationals, foreign nationals, SAARC, concessions), and audit-ready data exports.
- Proof point: Government museum-specific feature set

## In-Article CTA Format (Blog Posts)

Every blog article includes one CTA section before the conclusion. Follow this structure:

**Section heading**: Use a natural H2 — not "Call to Action" or "Get Started"
- ✅ "See how EveryTicket manages this for your museum"
- ✅ "How EveryTicket handles this at Indian museums"

**Body** (2–4 sentences):
1. Connect to the article's topic — reference the specific capability discussed
2. State what EveryTicket does about it, using "we"
3. End with a natural invitation to demo — no exclamation marks, no urgency language

**Link format**: Anchor text should be a natural phrase — not "click here" or "schedule a demo now"
- ✅ "schedule a demo and we will walk you through the setup"
- ✅ "see how it works for your museum"

## Landing Page Structure

### For Museum Ticketing Software Landing Pages

1. **Hero**: Outcome-first headline + subheadline naming the audience + "Schedule a Demo" CTA + one trust signal (ticket volume or named deployment)
2. **Pain section**: 3 specific problems Indian museum administrators face (queues, fake tickets, manual reconciliation, GST errors, counter hours)
3. **Solution section**: How EveryTicket addresses each pain — specific features, not vague claims
4. **Social proof**: MAP Bangalore deployment, 150,000+ tickets, 40% wait time reduction
5. **Feature breakdown**: Online booking, POS, kiosks, CRM, analytics — benefit-led, not technical
6. **Pricing**: All four plans with INR pricing — no "contact us for pricing" for the first three tiers
7. **Objection section**: FAQ addressing the 5–7 most common objections (see above)
8. **Closing CTA**: Demo booking with risk reversal directly below

### For Location-Specific Pages (e.g., "Museum Ticketing Software in Bangalore")
Same structure, with:
- City name in headline and first paragraph
- India-specific trust signals (MAP Bangalore deployment)
- Local institution language (state government museums, heritage sites)
- UPI and GST compliance highlighted early — local audience expects it

## Mobile Optimisation

- 60%+ of demo research happens on mobile
- Full-width CTA buttons on screens under 768px
- Headline under 60 characters for mobile
- No horizontal scrolling
- Tap targets minimum 44x44px
- Form fields: large enough to type without zooming (16px+ font)
- Accordion FAQs to reduce scroll depth on mobile

## Form Optimisation (Demo Request)

### Recommended Fields
| Goal | Fields | What to Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Demo request | 3–4 | Name, Email, Museum/Organisation Name, (Role) |
| Contact us | 2–3 | Name, Email, Message |

Every additional field reduces form completions. Keep demo forms to 4 fields maximum.

### Form Best Practices
- Single-column layout
- Clear label above each field
- Placeholder text that explains what to enter
- Submit button: "Schedule My Demo" (not "Submit")
- Risk reversal directly below submit button: "No commitment. Free 20-minute session."

## Pricing Page CRO

### Display Principles
- Show all three public plans (Starter ₹7,000 / Essential ₹11,000 / Institutional ₹15,000) with exact INR pricing
- Highlight "most popular" on the Essential or Institutional plan
- Show what is included in each plan — not just what it costs
- Place "No per-ticket commissions. No hidden fees." prominently on the pricing page
- Custom plan CTA: "Talk to our team" (not "Contact us" — too cold)

### What Pricing Page Must Answer
1. What is included in each plan?
2. Are there per-ticket fees? (Answer: No)
3. Is there a long-term contract? (Answer: No)
4. What happens if we exceed the ticket limit?
5. Is GST charged on the subscription?

## A/B Testing Priorities for EveryTicket

### High Impact (Test First)
1. Hero headline — outcome vs. audience-specific vs. social proof formula
2. CTA text — "Schedule a Demo" vs. "Book a Free Demo" vs. "See EveryTicket in Action"
3. Trust signal above fold — ticket volume vs. named deployment vs. wait time reduction stat
4. Risk reversal copy — "No contracts" vs. "Free training" vs. "60-minute setup"
5. Demo form length — 3 fields vs. 4 fields

### Medium Impact
6. Pricing plan highlight (which plan to mark as "most popular")
7. Testimonial selection — operational outcome vs. ease of use vs. India-specific
8. Hero image — product dashboard vs. museum entry scene vs. kiosk in use
9. FAQ section placement — mid-page vs. near CTA

### Testing Rules
- One variable at a time
- Minimum 50 demo request conversions per variant before calling a winner
- Run for at least 3 weeks (B2B conversion cycles are slower)
- 95% statistical significance

## Conversion Killers to Avoid

1. Generic headlines that do not state a specific outcome
2. CTAs that say "Submit," "Click here," or "Contact us"
3. No trust signal above the fold
4. Pricing hidden behind "contact sales" for all tiers
5. Long demo request forms (more than 4 fields)
6. No risk reversal near the CTA
7. Slow page load (every 1-second delay drops conversions 7%)
8. Em dashes (—) and flowery language in CTA copy (inconsistent with article voice)
9. Competitor names in any page copy (per competitor-analysis.md rules)
10. Mobile-unfriendly tap targets or unreadable font sizes
