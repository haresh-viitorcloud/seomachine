# EveryTicket Style Guide

This guide defines writing conventions, formatting standards, and editorial rules for all EveryTicket content — blog articles, landing pages, and CTAs.

---

## Voice & Tone

### Core Voice
EveryTicket content is written from the perspective of the **marketing head of EveryTicket** — someone with direct operational experience running ticketing deployments at Indian museums. The voice is:

- **Confident and authoritative**: We write from experience, not from research
- **Direct and practical**: Every sentence states something useful
- **India-aware**: We understand the Indian museum context — GST, UPI, government procurement, ASI institutions
- **Not promotional**: We persuade through facts and outcomes, not adjectives

### Tone by Content Type
- **Blog articles**: First-person "I" — written as the marketing head sharing expertise
- **CTA sections**: "We" — EveryTicket speaks as a team offering a capability
- **Feature descriptions**: Active, benefit-led, third-person factual
- **Meta descriptions**: Direct answer to the query, second-person ("you")

## Writing Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These rules apply to every piece of EveryTicket content without exception.

### Banned Sentence Structures
- **"Not just X, but Y"** — never use this construction
  - ❌ "Not just a ticketing tool, but a full management suite"
  - ✅ "EveryTicket manages ticketing, POS, CRM, and analytics in one dashboard"
- **"We don't just X; we Y"** — never use this construction
  - ❌ "We don't just process tickets; we transform your museum operations"
  - ✅ "EveryTicket processes tickets, manages entry, and tracks visitor data"
- Contrasting metaphors used to explain simple, literal tasks
- Dramatic transitions ("In today's fast-paced world...", "Now more than ever...")

### Banned Punctuation
- **Em dash (—)**: Never use in article body copy. Use a comma, period, or rewrite the sentence.
  - ❌ "EveryTicket — built for Indian museums — starts at ₹7,000/month"
  - ✅ "EveryTicket is built for Indian museums and starts at ₹7,000/month"

### Banned Language
- Flowery or decorative language: "seamlessly," "revolutionary," "game-changing," "transformative," "powerful"
- Idioms and metaphors: "tip of the iceberg," "move the needle," "low-hanging fruit"
- Filler qualifiers: "very," "really," "truly," "actually," "basically"
- Vague claims without numbers: "saves time," "improves efficiency," "boosts revenue" — always quantify

### Plain English Standards
- **Active voice**: 80%+ of sentences. State who does what.
  - ❌ "Tickets are processed by the system in real time"
  - ✅ "The system processes tickets in real time"
- **Short sentences**: 15–20 words on average. Break anything over 25 words into two sentences.
- **One idea per paragraph**: 2–4 sentences. No walls of text.
- **Direct statements over narrative descriptions**: Say what happens, not how it feels

### Competitor References
- **Never name competitors** in any EveryTicket content
- When contrast is needed, use: "generic international platforms," "per-ticket fee models," "Western ticketing tools," or "traditional manual systems"
- Never use competitor case studies or examples — even positively framed

## Grammar & Mechanics

### Capitalisation

**Headlines and Subheadings**: Sentence case
- ✅ "How timed entry reduces museum queues"
- ❌ "How Timed Entry Reduces Museum Queues"

**Product and Feature Names**:
- EveryTicket (always capital E and T, one word)
- Starter, Essential, Institutional, Custom (plan names — always capitalised)
- POS (always caps), EPOS (always caps), CRM (always caps), QR (always caps), UPI (always caps), GST (always caps)

**Industry Terms**:
- museum (lowercase unless start of sentence or part of a proper name)
- ticketing software, ticketing system, ticketing platform (all lowercase)
- digital ticketing, online ticketing (lowercase)
- India, Indian (always capitalised)
- GST, UPI, ASI, SAARC (always caps — Indian regulatory and institutional acronyms)

### Numbers

**Spell out vs. numerals**:
- Spell out: one through nine
- Numerals: 10 and above
- Always numerals for: percentages (40%), money (₹7,000), ticket counts (150,000), time (60 minutes), measurements

**Indian currency**:
- Use ₹ symbol: ₹7,000 (not "Rs 7,000" or "INR 7,000")
- Use Indian comma formatting: ₹7,000 / ₹1,00,000

**Large numbers**:
- 150,000 (not "1.5 lakh" in English content — use international format)
- "over 150,000 tickets" not "150,000+ tickets" in running prose (use + in headlines/bullets only)

### Punctuation

**Oxford comma**: Yes — always use it
- ✅ "online booking, POS, and kiosk sales"

**Em dash**: Banned (see Writing Rules above)

**Ellipses**: Avoid in editorial content. Use only when quoting truncated text.

**Quotation marks**: Use for direct quotes and named article/report titles only. Not for emphasis.

**Hyphens**:
- Use in compound modifiers before a noun: "museum-specific software," "India-first platform," "GST-compliant billing"
- Do not hyphenate after a verb: "the software is GST compliant," "the platform is mobile friendly"

### Abbreviations & Acronyms

Spell out on first use, then use acronym in parentheses — except for universally known terms:
- Point of Sale (POS) → POS thereafter
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM) → CRM thereafter
- Universally known — no need to spell out: QR, UPI, GST, URL, SEO, API

**Common EveryTicket acronyms**:
- POS: Point of Sale
- EPOS: Electronic Point of Sale
- CRM: Customer Relationship Management
- UPI: Unified Payments Interface
- GST: Goods and Services Tax
- ASI: Archaeological Survey of India
- QR: Quick Response (code)

## Word Choice

### Preferred Terms

| Say This | Not That |
|---|---|
| museum administrator | museum manager / operator |
| visitor | guest (in museum context) |
| ticket category | ticket type (for consistency with EveryTicket UI) |
| timed entry | time-slot booking |
| walk-in visitor | on-site visitor |
| EveryTicket | the platform / the system / the software (use the name) |
| deploy / go live | implement / launch (in setup context) |
| on-premises | on-site (when referring to physical location) |
| digital ticket | e-ticket (prefer "digital ticket") |
| schedule a demo | book a demo (both acceptable; "schedule" preferred in CTAs) |

### Words to Avoid in All Content
- "seamlessly" — overused filler
- "robust" — vague, means nothing specific
- "leverage" — use "use" or "apply"
- "innovative," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art" — unsubstantiated claims
- "solution" as a standalone noun — say what the solution does
- "empowers" — use direct statements instead
- "click here," "read more," "check this out" — use descriptive anchor text

### India-Specific Language
- Use "museum administrator" or "museum management team" — not "museum operator" (sounds commercial)
- Refer to government institutions as "government museums" or "public museums" — not "state-owned museums"
- "Heritage site" or "heritage museum" for ASI-listed properties
- Use ₹ (not USD) in all India-focused pricing content

## Formatting Standards

### Article Body

**Bold**:
- Use for key terms on first introduction, important figures, and critical warnings
- Maximum 1–2 bold instances per paragraph
- Never bold entire sentences

**Italics**:
- Use for titles of reports, publications, or studies
- Use sparingly for genuine emphasis — not for decoration

**Underline**: Never use (reserved for hyperlinks in web context)

**ALL CAPS**: Only for standard acronyms (POS, CRM, UPI, GST, QR). Never for emphasis.

### Lists

Use bullet points and numbered lists **only when genuinely needed**:
- 3 or more parallel items that would be awkward in a sentence
- Step-by-step processes where sequence matters
- Feature sets where each item stands independently

**Do not** default to bullets to avoid writing sentences. A short, well-constructed paragraph is preferable to an unnecessary list.

**Bullet point style**:
- Sentence case, first word capitalised
- End with a period if it is a complete sentence; no punctuation if it is a fragment
- Parallel structure across all items in a list (all fragments or all sentences — not mixed)
- Maximum 2 levels of nesting

**Numbered lists**: Use for sequential steps only. Format: `1.` with a period.

### Links

**Internal links**:
- Short, natural anchor text that fits the sentence — typically 2–5 words
- Never use the full blog post title as the anchor
- Never introduce with "you can check this," "click here," "see here," or "read more about"
- ✅ "timed entry reduces queues by up to 40%"
- ❌ "You can read more about this in our article How Museum Timed Entry Reduces Visitor Queue Times"

**External links**:
- 2 per article, in separate paragraphs and separate sections
- Authority sources only: government bodies, established research institutions, major national newspapers (The Hindu, Times of India), ICOM, UNESCO
- No competitor websites

### Headings

**H1**: One per article. Sentence case. News-style (see seo-guidelines.md). Primary keyword near the start.

**H2**: 4–6 per article. Sentence case. News-style subheadings — engaging and informative, not just labels.
- ✅ "Why Indian museums lose revenue at the counter every day"
- ❌ "Revenue issues"

**H3**: Nested under H2. Concise and specific. Never skip from H2 to H4.

### Numbers in Statistics

Always pair statistics with a source or context:
- ✅ "EveryTicket has processed over 150,000 tickets across museum deployments"
- ✅ "Museums using timed entry reduced visitor wait times by up to 40%"
- ❌ "Museums see massive improvements in efficiency"

**EveryTicket's own proof points** (use these in content):
- 150,000+ tickets processed
- 54,000+ online bookings
- Wait time reduction of up to 40%
- Deployment at MAP (Museum of Art & Photography), Bangalore
- Online portal goes live in 60 minutes
- Full system deployed within 30 days

## Content Structure

### Article Introduction (100–150 words)
1. **Direct answer or context** (1–2 sentences): State what the article addresses — primary keyword in first 100 words
2. **The problem** (2–3 sentences): What operational or institutional challenge does this cover?
3. **What the reader will get** (1–2 sentences): What will they know or be able to do after reading?

Do not open with a dramatic question, a rhetorical hook, or a historical anecdote. Open with direct, relevant information.

### Key Takeaways Block
Place after the introduction, before the first H2 body section. Format as a blockquote:

```
> **Key Takeaways**
> - [Specific finding or recommendation]
> - [Specific finding or recommendation]
> - [Specific finding or recommendation]
```

3–5 bullets. Each is a standalone, complete claim — not a teaser.

### Section Format
- Each H2 section covers one idea
- Use bullet points or H3 sub-sections within each H2 — avoid long prose paragraphs
- One internal link per section maximum; no two links in the same paragraph

### CTA Section (before conclusion)
- Placed as a dedicated section before the conclusion
- Uses "we" — EveryTicket speaks as a team
- Grounded in a specific capability or outcome from the article
- 2–4 sentences maximum
- No exclamation marks, no urgency language ("Don't miss out!"), no generic pitches

### Conclusion (100–150 words)
- Recap key points (3–5 takeaways)
- Primary keyword in the conclusion
- Clear next step for the reader

### FAQ Section (always last — after Conclusion)
- Section heading: `## FAQs` — never "Frequently asked questions" or any other variation
- Each question: `### H3` heading
- Each answer: plain body paragraph — never bold, never a list item
- 4–6 questions per article; answers 15–20 words each, direct answer in the first sentence
- FAQs must always be the **final section** of the article, placed after the Conclusion

## SEO-Specific Style

### Frontmatter (meta elements storage)

All meta elements go in the **YAML frontmatter block** at the very top of the draft file, before the H1. Never place them as `**Bold**: Value` lines in the article body — the publisher reads only the frontmatter block, and anything in the body will appear in the WordPress post.

```yaml
---
title: "[Article title]"
meta_title: "[50-60 char]"
meta_description: "[150-160 char]"
primary_keyword: [keyword]
secondary_keywords: [kw1, kw2]
url_slug: /blog/[slug]
author: Marketing Head, EveryTicket
last_updated: [DD Month YYYY]
word_count: ~1,200
internal_links:
 - [URL]
external_links:
 - [URL] ([source])
---
```

### Meta Titles (`meta_title`)
- 50–60 characters including spaces
- Sentence case
- Primary keyword early in the title
- Include current year for time-sensitive topics
- No trailing "| EveryTicket" unless character count allows

### Meta Descriptions (`meta_description`)
- 150–160 characters
- Directly answers the search query in the first clause
- Includes primary keyword naturally
- Ends with an action phrase
- Must be a complete sentence — never cut off mid-thought

### URL Slugs (`url_slug`)
- Lowercase, hyphens only (no underscores)
- Primary keyword in the slug
- 3–6 words
- Format: `/blog/primary-keyword-phrase`
- No stop words unless essential for readability
- The publisher strips the `/blog/` prefix when setting the WordPress slug

### Image Alt Text
- Describe what the image shows — do not start with "image of" or "photo of"
- Include the primary keyword naturally if relevant
- 125 characters or less
- ✅ "Museum entry QR code scanning at EveryTicket kiosk in Bangalore"
- ❌ "Image" or "Screenshot"

## Dates, Times, and Currency

**Dates**: DD Month YYYY — 10 May 2026

**Times**: 12-hour format with a.m./p.m. — 10:30 a.m.

**Time zones**: IST for India-specific content; specify when relevant for international context

**Currency**: ₹ symbol — ₹7,000 / ₹11,000 / ₹15,000. No space between ₹ and the number.
