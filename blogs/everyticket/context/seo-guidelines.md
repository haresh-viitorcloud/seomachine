# SEO Guidelines for EveryTicket Content

This document outlines SEO requirements and writing standards for all EveryTicket blog content. Guidelines are derived from the two-phase content workflow (Research → Write) used to produce articles for everyticket.in.

---

## Content Length

- **Standard article**: 1,200 words
- **Target**: Hit 1,200 words accurately — not significantly over or under
- Every sentence must earn its place; no padding to reach word count
- If a topic genuinely requires more depth, break it into a series of related articles rather than exceeding the limit

---

## Two-Phase Workflow

All EveryTicket articles follow a structured two-phase process:

### Phase 1: Research
Before writing, research the following:
1. Identify which topic cluster the article belongs to using `context/target-keywords.md` and confirm the primary + supporting keywords to target
2. The target topic and search intent of the target industry audience in the target geography
3. Authority external sources — identify **1 external authority link** to support a key claim
4. EveryTicket's website (everyticket.in) to find **internal blog and capability links** relevant to the topic
5. Existing published articles on everyticket.in to confirm there is no content overlap with what is being written
6. Check the cannibalization watch table in `context/target-keywords.md` — confirm no existing article already targets the same keyword
7. Produce a proposed article structure (outline with H2/H3 headings) before writing begins

### Phase 2: Writing
Write the full article following the structure, keywords, and links identified in Phase 1. Apply all style and formatting rules below.

---

## Keyword Requirements

- **Reference**: See `context/target-keywords.md` for the full keyword list organised by topic cluster
- **Minimum frequency**: Every target keyword for the article must appear **at least 4 times**
- Cover all provided target keywords — none can be skipped
- Use natural keyword variations (do not repeat the exact phrase robotically)
- Primary keyword must appear in: H1 title, first 100 words, at least 2 H2 subheadings, meta title, meta description, and URL slug
- **No keyword stuffing**: Integration must read naturally
- **Cannibalization check**: Before targeting a keyword, verify against the cannibalization watch table in `context/target-keywords.md` to ensure no existing article already targets it. Two articles targeting the same keyword will compete against each other in search results.

## Content Structure

### Heading Hierarchy

#### H1 (Title)
- One H1 per article
- Include primary keyword naturally
- Write in a news headline style: engaging, specific, and direct — modelled on The Times of India or The New York Times
- Should make the reader want to continue reading immediately

#### H2 (Main Sections)
- 4–6 H2 sections for a 1,200-word article
- Each H2 title should read like a news sub-headline: informative and engaging on its own
- At least 2–3 H2s should include keyword variations
- Subheadings must entice the reader to read the explanation beneath them — they are not just labels

#### H3 (Subsections)
- Use within H2s when breaking a section into distinct sub-points
- Never skip from H2 to H4
- Keep concise and specific

### Section Content Format
- Explanations under subheadings should be in **bullet points or H3s**, not long paragraphs
- Avoid walls of text under any heading
- One clear idea per section — do not blend multiple topics within a single H2/H3

### Article Structure Template

```markdown
# [H1: News-style title with primary keyword]

[Introduction: 100–150 words — state the context and what the article covers. Primary keyword in first 100 words.]

## [H2: Section 1 — keyword variation where natural]
[Bullet points or H3s. Concise.]

## [H2: Section 2]
[Bullet points or H3s. Concise.]

## [H2: Section 3 — keyword variation]
[Bullet points or H3s. Concise.]

## [H2: Section 4]
[Bullet points or H3s. Concise.]

## [H2: EveryTicket CTA section — natural, capability-backed, before conclusion]
[Short paragraph using "we". Not a sales pitch — grounded in capabilities and outcomes.]

## Conclusion
[100–150 words. Recap key points. Include primary keyword. Reinforce value.]

## FAQs

### [Question 1?]
[Answer — direct, 15–20 words.]

### [Question 2?]
[Answer — direct, 15–20 words.]

### [Question 3?]
[Answer — direct, 15–20 words.]

### [Question 4?]
[Answer — direct, 15–20 words.]

### [Question 5?]
[Answer — direct, 15–20 words.]
```

**FAQ rules:**
- FAQs are always the **last section** of every article — after the Conclusion
- The section heading is always `## FAQs` (not "Frequently asked questions")
- Each question is an `### H3` heading
- Answers are plain body text — never bold, never in a list
- 4–6 questions per article; 15–20 word answers

## Writing Style

### Plain English Standards
- Short sentences. Target 15–20 words per sentence. Break longer sentences into two.
- Active voice. 80%+ of sentences should use active voice.
- Direct, literal language. Say exactly what you mean.
- No marketing-speak, dramatic transitions, or dramatic openers.

### Banned Sentence Structures
The following patterns must never appear in EveryTicket articles:
- "Not just X, but Y" (e.g., "Not just a ticketing tool, but a full management suite")
- "We don't just X; we Y" (e.g., "We don't just process tickets; we transform experiences")
- Contrasting metaphors used to explain simple tasks
- Flowery language, idioms, or abstract metaphors
- Narrative descriptions where direct statements would work

### Banned Punctuation
- **No em dashes (—)** anywhere in the article body

### Tone
- Write from the perspective of EveryTicket's marketing head (first-person "I" is appropriate for the author voice throughout the article body)
- The article should project EveryTicket's own expertise, experience, and case knowledge — not feel like a third-party research piece
- Confident and persuasive without being promotional
- Matter-of-fact and authoritative

### Bullet Points
- Use bullet points only when genuinely needed — a list of 3 or more parallel items, steps, or features
- Do not default to bullets to avoid writing sentences
- Prefer bullets or H3s over long paragraphs within any section

## Internal Linking

### Requirements
- **Minimum**: 3 internal links per article
- **Optimal**: 4–5 internal links
- Links must be distributed evenly throughout the article — never clustered in one section or paragraph

### Anchor Text Rules
- Use **short, natural anchor text** that fits the sentence
- Never use the full blog post title as the anchor (e.g., do not link "How to Choose the Right Museum Ticketing Software" — instead link "choose the right ticketing software")
- Do not introduce links with phrases like "you can review this," "check this out," or "see here" — the link must live naturally inside a real sentence
- Vary anchor text across the article; do not repeat the same anchor for the same destination

### What to Link To
- EveryTicket blog posts relevant to the topic being covered
- EveryTicket service/capability pages where contextually relevant
- Reference `context/internal-links-map.md` for the full map of available links

### What Not to Link To
- Competitor websites
- Same-level service provider companies
- Low-authority third-party sources (external links are handled separately)

---

## External Linking

### Requirements
- **Exactly 2 external authority links** per article
- Both links must be placed naturally — not in the same paragraph and not in the same section
- Each link must directly support a specific claim, statistic, or fact in the article

### Quality Standards
- Authority sources only: government bodies, established research institutions, well-known industry publications (e.g., UNESCO, India Ministry of Culture, ICOM, major national newspapers)
- Prefer sources published within the last 2 years for data and statistics
- No competitor or same-level service provider sites
- No random blogs or low-domain-authority sources

---

## CTA Requirements

- One CTA for EveryTicket, placed **before the conclusion**
- Must use **"we"** when referring to EveryTicket in this section
- Must be grounded in EveryTicket's capabilities and outcomes — not a generic sales pitch
- Should read as a natural continuation of the article, not a sudden gear-shift into marketing
- The CTA should feel like an expert recommendation based on what was discussed in the article

---

## Competitor & Competitor-Adjacent Content Rules

- **Never mention competitors** by name in EveryTicket articles
- **Never use competitor case studies or examples** — even positively framed
- **Never reference same-level service providers** as examples or comparisons
- If a comparison is needed to make a point, frame it as "traditional/manual systems" vs. digital — not named companies

---

## Content Overlap Prevention

Before writing, verify:
1. Search everyticket.in/blog for existing articles on the same or closely related topic
2. Review the proposed article outline against published content to confirm no section duplicates existing material
3. If overlap exists, either choose a different angle or closely related sub-topic, or confirm the new article adds distinct value (e.g., different geo focus, different audience segment, updated year)

## Meta Elements

All meta elements are stored in the **YAML frontmatter block** at the top of every draft file, before the H1. They must never appear as `**Bold**: Value` lines in the article body — those would be sent to WordPress as visible post content.

### YAML Frontmatter Format

```yaml
---
title: "[Full article title matching H1]"
meta_title: "[50-60 char SEO title]"
meta_description: "[150-160 char meta description]"
primary_keyword: [primary keyword phrase]
secondary_keywords: [keyword1, keyword2]
url_slug: /blog/[primary-keyword-slug]
author: Marketing Head, EveryTicket
last_updated: [DD Month YYYY]
word_count: ~1,200
internal_links:
 - [full URL]
external_links:
 - [full URL] ([source name])
---
```

The WordPress publisher reads YAML frontmatter directly. The block is stripped before content is published — it will not appear in the WordPress post body.

### Meta Title (`meta_title`)
- 50–60 characters including spaces
- Primary keyword included, ideally near the start
- Sentence case: specific, benefit-clear, engaging
- Include the current year for time-sensitive topics

**Format options:**
- `[Primary Keyword]: [Clear Outcome or Benefit]`
- `How [topic] [achieves outcome] in [Year]`

### Meta Description (`meta_description`)
- 150–160 characters
- Directly answers the target search query
- Includes primary keyword and a clear action phrase
- Must not cut off mid-sentence

**Formula:**
```
[Direct answer to the query]. [Key benefit or differentiator]. [CTA phrase].
```

### URL Slug (`url_slug`)
- Lowercase, hyphens only
- Include primary keyword
- 3–6 words
- No stop words unless essential
- Format: `/blog/[primary-keyword-phrase]`
- The publisher strips the `/blog/` prefix automatically when setting the WordPress slug

## Readability Standards

- **Reading level**: 8th–10th grade (Flesch-Kincaid)
- **Sentence length**: 15–20 words average; break anything over 25 words
- **Paragraph length**: 2–4 sentences; single idea per paragraph
- **Subheadings**: Every 300–400 words of body text
- **White space**: No walls of text; mobile readability is a priority

---

## AI Search Optimisation (GEO)

- Place a direct answer to the target query within the first 1–2 sentences of the article — do not bury the answer
- Include a Key Takeaways block (3–5 bullets) after the introduction and before the first H2
- FAQ section (`## FAQs`) placed **after the Conclusion** — always the last section. 4–6 questions as `### H3` headings, plain body text answers (15–20 words each, never bold)
- Each H2/H3 section covers one idea only — this makes individual sections citable by AI engines
- Named author attribution on every article; include last-updated date
- Include the current year in the title for time-sensitive topics

---

## E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)

- Write from EveryTicket's direct experience — reference real deployments, outcomes, and operational knowledge
- Use specific numbers where available (e.g., 150,000+ tickets processed, 40% reduction in wait times, MAP Bangalore deployment)
- Back claims with data; cite sources for all statistics via the 2 external authority links
- Do not overpromise; be accurate about what the platform does and who it is for
- Named author (marketing head of EveryTicket) — not a generic byline
