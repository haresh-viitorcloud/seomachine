# ViitorCloud Style Guide

This guide defines writing conventions, formatting standards, and editorial guidelines for all ViitorCloud blog content.

---

## Writing Philosophy

Every article published on viitorcloud.com is authored from an expert account. Write in first-person ("I") as a practitioner sharing direct experience — not as a content writer summarizing research. The article should reflect ViitorCloud's expertise, project experience, and capabilities. It must feel confident and persuasive, not academic or neutral.

**Core writing principle**: Literal, matter-of-fact, direct. Write what happened, what works, and what to do — in plain English.

---

## Grammar & Mechanics

### Capitalization

**Headlines & Subheadings**: Title Case (Capitalize Major Words)

- Example: "How AI Is Changing the Way Logistics Companies Operate"

**Product & Company Names**:

- ViitorCloud: Always capitalized exactly as shown
- Reference specific services with natural capitalization: "custom AI development", "SaaS product engineering"

**Industry Terms**:

- AI: Always uppercase
- SaaS: Always uppercase
- API: Always uppercase
- IoT: Always as shown
- GenAI: Always as shown

### Numbers

**When to Spell Out**:

- Spell out: One through nine
- Use numerals: 10 and above
- Exceptions:
  - Percentages: Always use numerals (5%, 50%)
  - Money: Always use numerals ($5M, $500K, $46.4M)
  - Measurements: Always use numerals (15K sensors, 2M QR codes)
  - Lists and stats: Always use numerals for scannability

**Large Numbers**:

- 1,000 (use comma separator)
- Write as: 1.2 million, $46M, 70M users (not 70,000,000)
- Use K for thousands in stats: 100K users, 50K downloads

### Punctuation

**Oxford Comma**: Yes — always use it

- Example: "AI consulting, custom development, and system integration"

**Em Dashes**: **Do not use em dashes (—) anywhere in articles.**

- Replace with a comma or period — do not substitute with a semicolon or colon
- A semicolon or colon used in place of an em dash is also not permitted
- This applies to all published article content, including body text and headings

**Colons and Semicolons in Headings**: **Do not use colons, semicolons, or em dashes in H1, H2, or H3 headings.**

- Rewrite the heading as a clean phrase, statement, or question instead
- Example: instead of "AI Development: What You Need to Know" write "What You Need to Know About AI Development"
- Example: instead of "Custom AI vs Off-the-Shelf: The Real Difference" write "Why Custom AI Outperforms Off-the-Shelf Tools"

**Hyphens**: Use for compound modifiers before nouns

- "AI-first architecture", "real-time data", "cloud-native platform"

**Quotation Marks**: Use straight double quotes for quotations and titles

**Ellipses**: Avoid. Use a new sentence instead.

### Abbreviations & Acronyms

**First Use**:

- Spell out on first use, acronym in parentheses
- Example: "Large Language Models (LLMs) have changed..."
- Exceptions for universally known terms: AI, SEO, URL, API, SaaS

**Latin Abbreviations**:

- Avoid e.g., i.e., etc. — write in plain English instead
- Instead of "e.g.": "for example" or "such as"
- Instead of "i.e.": "that is" or "specifically"
- Instead of "etc.": list the items or write "and more"

## Voice & Tone

### Core Voice: Expert Practitioner

ViitorCloud articles are written from the perspective of an industry expert with hands-on experience. The author has built AI systems, delivered SaaS products, and worked with enterprise clients. Write from that position.

- Use "I" throughout — these articles come from an expert author account
- State observations and recommendations directly
- Reference ViitorCloud's actual project work where relevant
- Be confident — do not hedge with "it might", "possibly", "in some cases" unless genuinely uncertain

### Tone by Content Type

- **Technical articles**: Precise, direct, grounded in real implementation experience
- **Strategy articles**: Authoritative, clear, backed by measurable outcomes
- **Industry trend articles**: Analytical, informed, forward-looking
- **How-to guides**: Step-by-step, practical, no fluff

### Tone Variations: What to Avoid

These tones are off-brand for ViitorCloud:

- Enthusiastic marketing-speak ("Transform your business with our cutting-edge solutions!")
- Overly academic neutrality (sounds like a white paper, not an expert)
- Soft-sell narrative (every paragraph leads to a product mention)
- Storytelling intros that delay the actual point

## Sentence-Level Writing Rules

These rules are mandatory for all ViitorCloud articles.

### 1. Plain English Standard

- Keep sentences short: target 15-20 words average, maximum 25 words
- Use active voice in 80%+ of sentences
- Avoid marketing-speak and dramatic transitions
- Say the thing directly — do not build up to it

### 2. Banned Sentence Structures

Never use these patterns:

- "Not just X, but Y" — Example: ~~"Not just a development firm, but a strategic partner"~~
- "We don't just X; we Y" — Example: ~~"We don't just build apps; we build experiences"~~
- "It's not about X, it's about Y" — Example: ~~"It's not about code, it's about transformation"~~
- Any variation of these contrasting structures

### 3. No Flowery Language, Idioms, or Metaphors

- Do not use figurative language to describe technical or business processes
- Do not use contrasting metaphors to explain simple tasks
- Example of what to avoid: ~~"AI is the bridge that connects your data to your destiny"~~
- Example of correct approach: "AI processes your data and surfaces patterns that inform decisions."

### 4. No Dramatic Transitions

- Do not use transitions like: "But here's the thing...", "And that's where the magic happens.", "Welcome to the future of..."
- Use standard connective transitions: "As a result", "This means", "For example", "In practice"

### 5. Direct Action Language

- Focus on what things do, not what they represent or symbolize
- Describe processes in terms of inputs, actions, and outputs
- Example: "The model analyzes 15,000 sensor readings per day and flags anomalies in real time." — not "The model keeps a watchful eye on your operations."

### 6. No Padding

- Every sentence must add information
- Remove: "It goes without saying...", "As we all know...", "In today's fast-paced world..."
- Remove: Restatements of what was just said in different words

## Word Choice & Usage

### Preferred Terms for ViitorCloud Content

**Say This** — **Not That**:

- AI development — artificial intelligence development (first use only, then AI)
- custom AI solutions — bespoke AI (too formal/British)
- build — develop (prefer shorter, more direct word)
- use — utilize (always prefer "use")
- help — assist (prefer the plainer word)
- start — commence / initiate
- show — demonstrate (unless demonstrating something specific)
- clients — customers (ViitorCloud uses "clients" in a B2B context)
- results — outcomes (use both; vary for natural reading)

### Words to Avoid

- "very", "really", "truly", "quite", "rather" — remove or restructure the sentence
- "leverage" as a verb (use "use" instead)
- "synergy", "ecosystem" (unless genuinely technical)
- "seamlessly" — almost always meaningless filler
- "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "next-generation" — too vague; name the specific advancement
- "click here", "read more", "check this out", "you can review this" — use natural anchor text only
- "game-changer", "revolutionary", "disruptive" — show the impact with numbers instead
- "empower" as filler — state what the tool/service actually does
- "world-class" — unverifiable; use a real proof point instead

### Inclusive and International Language

- Use gender-neutral language: "they" instead of "he/she"
- Avoid idioms that may not translate globally (ViitorCloud serves international clients)
- Be mindful of cultural references that may not be universally understood

## Formatting Standards

### Text Formatting

**Bold**:

- Use for key statistics, critical warnings, and terms being defined
- Do not bold entire sentences or use bold for emphasis on every other phrase
- Example: "The platform processed **56,943 orders in 2024**."

**Italics**:

- Use for titles of publications, films, or named studies
- Avoid for general emphasis

**Underline**: Never — reserved for hyperlinks only

**ALL CAPS**: Only for standard acronyms (AI, SaaS, API, SEO, URL)

### Lists

**Bullet Points — When to Use**:

- Use for 3 or more related items, features, or steps
- Use when a section would otherwise become a long paragraph
- Do not force bullet points — if items flow naturally as prose, write them as prose

**Bullet Point Formatting**:

- Capitalize the first word of each item
- Use a period at the end if the item is a complete sentence; no period for fragments
- Keep parallel structure: all fragments or all complete sentences within a list
- Maximum 6-8 bullets before breaking into sub-sections

**Numbered Lists**:

- Use for sequential steps, processes, or ranked priorities only
- Same capitalization and punctuation rules as bullets

**Nested Lists**:

- Maximum 2 levels deep
- Use sparingly — if content needs 3 levels of nesting, restructure it

### Long Paragraphs: Prohibited

- Never write a paragraph longer than 4 sentences
- If content requires more than 4 sentences to explain, break it into bullet points or an H3 subsection
- This applies throughout the article — no exceptions for introductions or conclusions

### Links

**Anchor Text**:

- Natural — reads as part of the sentence, not as a label for the link
- Keyword-rich where possible (2-5 words)
- Never: "click here", "this link", "read more", "check this", "you can review this"
- Always: a natural phrase the reader would understand without the link being there

**Internal Links**:

- Spread across the full article — not clustered in one section
- Draw from `context/internal-links-map.md` for available ViitorCloud pages

**External Links**:

- Exactly 2 per standard article, in different sections
- Only to established authority sources (government, academic, major industry publications)
- Never to competitor websites or same-level IT companies

## Content Structure

### Article Introduction

**Structure** (100-200 words):

1. **Direct answer or thesis** (1-2 sentences): State the article's core point immediately — include the focus keyword
2. **Problem context** (2-3 sentences): What challenge or question does this address?
3. **What the article covers** (1-2 sentences): Set expectations

**Note**: The introduction must place the focus keyword in the first 100 words. The direct answer comes before the hook or narrative setup.

### Key Takeaways Block

After the introduction, before the first H2 section, include:

```markdown
> **Key Takeaways**
> - [Core finding #1 — specific, complete claim]
> - [Core finding #2]
> - [Core finding #3]
> - [Core finding #4 — optional]
```

### Section Length and Presentation

- **Minimum**: 100 words per section
- **Maximum**: 350 words per section — break into H3 subsections if longer
- **Default**: Use bullet points or H3 breakdowns for sections covering multiple points
- Long prose paragraphs in body sections are discouraged

### Subheading Title Style

Subheadings must engage the reader — write them like New York Times or Times of India article section titles.

- Create curiosity or imply a finding
- Avoid generic labels ("Benefits", "Overview", "Introduction")
- Example: Instead of "Benefits of Custom AI Development", write "Why Off-the-Shelf AI Falls Short for Most Businesses"
- The subheading should make the reader want to read the paragraph underneath it

### CTA Section (Before Conclusion)

- Must appear as a natural section before the conclusion
- Written as an expert recommendation backed by real ViitorCloud data or project outcomes
- Reference specific capabilities, client results, or metrics from `context/features.md`
- Must not read as a sales pitch

### Conclusion

**Structure** (100-150 words):

1. **Recap**: 2-3 key takeaways from the article (brief)
2. **Action**: What the reader should do with this information
3. **Forward-looking close**: One concise final thought (optional)

### FAQ Section (After Conclusion)

- FAQs go at the very end of the article, **after the conclusion**, not before
- 4-6 questions written in natural prompt language
- Each answer is direct in the first sentence (40-60 words total, or per article spec)

**Section order** for every article: Intro → Key Takeaways → body H2s → CTA → Conclusion → FAQ.

## SEO-Specific Style

### Meta Titles

- 50-60 characters including spaces
- Focus keyword near the beginning
- Journalistic headline style — not a label
- No ending punctuation
- Do not include "| ViitorCloud" unless space allows and keyword fits

### Meta Descriptions

- 150-160 characters
- Focus keyword included naturally
- Directly answers the target query (for AI search pickup)
- Ends with a call-to-action word or phrase

### URL Slugs

- Lowercase only
- Hyphens between words
- Include primary focus keyword
- 3-5 words ideal
- Format: `/blog/primary-keyword-phrase`

### Image Alt Text

- Include focus keyword naturally in at least one image's alt text
- Describe what the image shows — do not start with "image of" or "picture of"
- 125 characters or less

## Statistics & Data

### Citing Sources

- Always cite external statistics with source attribution
- Format: "According to [Source], [statistic]."
- Link to the original source
- Include year: "In 2024..."

### Using ViitorCloud's Own Data

- Prefer ViitorCloud project data over external statistics where available
- Draw from `context/features.md` for real client outcomes and metrics
- These are more credible and differentiated than generic industry stats

### Presenting Numbers

- Round large numbers for readability: "1.2 million" not "1,234,567"
- Use % symbol: 15% (not "percent")
- Use $ for money: $46.4M, $192M
- Include commas for thousands: 56,943 not 56943

## Images & Media

### Image Requirements

- At least one image per article, placed to break up text
- File name: descriptive and keyword-rich (e.g., `ai-development-services-india.jpg`)
- Alt text: includes focus keyword naturally; describes the image content

### Screenshots and Diagrams

- Include when they illustrate a specific process or result being discussed
- Crop to show only the relevant portion
- Alt text should describe the specific finding shown

## Brand-Specific Guidelines

### ViitorCloud References

- "ViitorCloud" — always capitalized exactly
- Do not write "Viitor Cloud" (two words) or "viitorcloud" (lowercase)
- When referencing services, use the exact service names from viitorcloud.com:
  - Custom AI Solutions
  - SaaS Product Engineering
  - AI-Driven Automation
  - System Integration & Modernization

### Competitor References

**Never name, cite, link to, or reference competitors or same-level IT companies** in any article. This includes using their case studies, statistics, or examples — even for comparison. Use generic references if necessary ("other AI vendors", "typical development agencies").

### Client and Case Study References

- Use real ViitorCloud client outcomes and project data from `context/features.md`
- Client names may be referenced only if publicly confirmed (MariDeal, LogixHealth, Globe 24-7, Save the Children, etc.)
- Do not invent or approximate client data

### Screen Reader Friendly

- Descriptive anchor text (never "click here")
- Image alt text for all images
- Proper heading hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3 — no skipping levels)

### Plain Language

- 8th-10th grade reading level target
- Define technical terms on first use
- Short sentences and paragraphs throughout
