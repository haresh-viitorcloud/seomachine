# Medium — Publishing Guide (for Repurposed Content)

**URL:** https://medium.com
**Type:** Large publishing network with its own reader base and a Partner Program that can pay you for reads.
**Repurposing verdict:** ✅ **Best-case platform.** Medium fully supports canonical links, so you can republish an article *in full* and have it strengthen — not cannibalize — your original.

---

## Account

Sign up at medium.com with Google, Facebook, Apple, or email. Free.

---

## Two ways to republish (pick based on the date you want)

### Method A — Import tool (fastest, auto-canonical)

1. Click your **profile picture → Stories**.
2. Click **Import a story** (top-right).
3. Paste the URL of your original post and click **Import**.
4. Medium pulls in the text and formatting **and automatically adds a `rel="canonical"` link back to the original**.
5. Click **See your story**, edit as needed, then **Publish**.

> Note: the import tool **backdates** the post to the original's publish date. Good for accuracy, but Medium treats it as an older story, so it gets less of a "fresh post" boost.

### Method B — Copy-paste + manual canonical (keeps today's date)

1. Click **Write**, paste your article, and **Publish** it.
2. On the published story, click the **⋯ (three dots) → Edit story** (or Story settings).
3. Go to **More settings → Advanced settings**.
4. Tick **"This story was originally published elsewhere."**
5. Enter your original URL and click **Save canonical link**.
6. Click **Publish** again to save the change.

> Because you publish first and add the canonical after, the post keeps **today's date** and Medium treats it as fresh — usually more views/reads. This is the method most people prefer for driving traffic.

**Only the author can set a canonical link.** Only import/repost content you have the rights to.

---

## Verify the canonical worked

On the published post, right-click → **View page source**, search for `canonical`. The first result should show **your original URL**, not the Medium URL.

---

## Formatting & media

- Rich editor: headings, bold/italic, quotes, code blocks, embeds (YouTube, tweets, Gists, etc.).
- **Add at least one image** near the top — it becomes the social-share preview and improves readability.
- You can tweak the imported copy to fit Medium's audience (e.g. a Medium-specific intro) without losing the canonical benefit.

## Tags

- Add **up to 5 tags** before publishing (dropdown next to Publish).
- Type a tag to see how often it's used; pick relevant, reasonably-used tags for discoverability.

## Publications (optional, more reach)

- You can submit imported posts to a **Publication** (yours or one that accepts submissions) for a larger audience. Add imported posts from your profile into your publication.

## Blog-writing instructions for Medium

- **Title + subtitle:** the title should promise a clear benefit or spark curiosity; the subtitle (dek) adds context. On import, your original H1 becomes the title and the next line can become the subtitle — check both read well on Medium.
- **Cover image:** add a strong, relevant image at the very top — it becomes the preview/social card and sets the tone.
- **Opening:** the first 1–2 paragraphs show as preview text and decide whether people keep reading. Put the hook there; don't bury it.
- **Structure:** use Medium's large/small section headers, short paragraphs (2–4 lines), block quotes for emphasis, and images/screenshots to break up long stretches.
- **Length:** 5–7 minute reads (~1,000–1,600 words) tend to perform well, but match the topic.
- **Voice:** conversational, first-person works well here.
- **Ending:** close with a one-line takeaway and a soft CTA (follow you, read the original on your site, subscribe).
- **Tags:** 5 specific, reasonably-used tags.
- **Repurposing tweak:** a short Medium-specific intro is fine — you can lightly adapt without losing the canonical benefit.

## Metadata & SEO tags

- **Canonical (most important):** set it to your original (see above) so search credits your site, not Medium. This is the key meta tag for re-posts.
- **SEO title + meta description:** in the draft, click **⋯ (three dots) → More settings → SEO Settings**. Edit the **SEO title** (~60 chars) and the **meta description** (~155 chars; Medium truncates around 156 desktop / 120 mobile). These affect only Google/social — your on-page title and subtitle stay as written. Put your target keyword in both, naturally.
- **URL slug:** in the same area, use **Customize Story Link → Custom** to get your keyword into the URL. Do this **before** publishing — changing it later breaks the old link.
- **Open Graph / social card:** built from your **title**, **subtitle/description**, and **cover image** — set a clear cover image so shares look right.
- Tags (5) aren't meta tags, but they're how Medium categorizes and distributes the story.
